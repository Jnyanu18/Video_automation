# Product marketing context: AskVerb

Read by the copywriting, copy-editing and marketing-psychology skills before any AskVerb script or caption. Source of
truth for positioning is Vishnu's saved notes and the Verb repo's docs/13-HANDOFF.md; this file is the short version.
Updated 2026-10-30.

## What it is

AskVerb (askverb.com) puts an AI agent inside someone else's SaaS product, so that product's users can say what they
want and have it done, acting as the signed-in user, with every change shown and approved before it runs. It runs on
the product's existing API, described in one file the team owns. Integrated in under an hour in most cases.

## Who it is for (from 2026-10-30)

Niche operations SaaS: logistics, warehouse (WMS), transport (TMS), quality (QMS), fleet, maintenance, field service,
compliance. About 40 to 50 people, a few million in ARR, dated architecture, no AI team, a public API. Their customers
are asking for AI and they cannot ship it themselves. The buyer is the founder, CEO or VP Product. Dropped: CRM,
scheduling, project management (they build their own), and booking.

## The pitch

AI in your product without an AI team. Lead short-form copy with what the agent does for the user (a real multi-step
task in their domain). Approval on every change and the audit trail are supporting detail, never the headline.
Examples: a shipment, a work order, a deviation, a pickup, a route. Never a booking.

## Offer

A free 30-day pilot: we set it up, track requests handled and tasks completed, then agree a price from what it handled.
It is not free forever. Never "free while we launch", never a price, never the daily limit.

## Never

- Support framing (tickets, deflection, helpdesk, support cost).
- A docs chatbot as the opponent ("a chatbot answers, Verb does" is retired).
- "Copilot", puns on the name, "15 minutes", invented customers, users or results.
- A booking example. A call or meeting ask in a first message.

## Proof we can use

- Live since 2026-08-21. One external live install (Resuelve API). 0 paying as of 2026-10-29: never imply customers.
- The demo install is cal.askverb.com (a scheduling app, our own copy of cal.diy, said plainly on camera): fine as
  proof that it acts, but its domain is not the target's.
- Rehearsed on 2026-10-27: the approval card appears in about 10 s, done in about 12 s.

## Angles to sell on (Vishnu, 2026-10-31)

One angle per reel; build many reels on each.

1. **FOMO.** Your competitor has AI, or soon will; if you don't ship one soon you fall behind. Here's how to ship it in a
   day.
2. **Trust and reliability.** How safe Verb is and that it is built for production (rate limits, a changed record holds
   the action back, every change approved), and what it would take to build that yourself: the engineering cost and the
   skills a team would need.
3. **Ease of integration.** How easy it is to set up, to test locally before pushing to production, and to add or upgrade
   tools over time.
4. **UX.** Moving users from clicking through flows to just asking inside the app is a big upgrade to the product
   itself.

Also: the users' point of view (video 1, askverb-ship-today) and the investors' point of view (parked:
videos/askverb-investors/script.md).

## Learnings

- Short-form copy that leads with "it asks permission" reads as a limitation (2026-08-20).
- The security-review / shippability angle is not the hook (2026-09-18).
