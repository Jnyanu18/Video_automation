What the scout ranks ideas against, and what every script, storyboard and render is checked against. It is a living
file: every yes, no and change he gives goes in the log at the bottom the same turn, and a pattern seen twice is
promoted to a rule above it (with the date and his words). Rules are replaced in place when he changes his mind,
never stacked.

## Rules (promoted from the log)

- **Hooks open on what the viewer gets** ("you", "your own"), or on a gap or comparison ("how devs at Anthropic use
  Claude and how everyone else does"). His own wording beats formulas. (2026-11-06)
- **Something new you can use today** beats news and explainers: a free tool, a skill, a setup. (2026-11-06, from the
  numbers above)
- **Tool and how-to reels end on a comment keyword** that sends a real .docx guide (2026-11-02). **Jaw-dropping
  news reels have no guide**: they end on "comment what you think" or "follow for more" (2026-11-07).
- **Directed like 34-three-agents**: a new picture on every phrase, real logos and mascots, our own take on real
  interfaces, white keynote look by default. (2026-11-06)
- **No 3D scenes.** No em dashes. No "not X, not Y" chains. (2026-11-02, 2026-10-29)

## What makes a scout pick worth offering

1. It beat its own channel at least 3x (the scout's "x normal" column). Raw views alone mean nothing.
2. It fits the audience above, or the format can be carried into it.
3. It has a twist only we can make: a true fact of ours, our studio, our tools, our look.
4. The facts can be checked from a primary source before it is said.
5. Two channels hitting the same topic in the same fortnight is a trend; say so.
