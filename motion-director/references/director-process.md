# The director's process, in detail

The quality of a motion film is decided before any code exists. Most weak films come from skipping steps 1–6 and starting at "which effects look cool".

## 1. Insight

Ask: what does the audience feel *before* they meet this product, and what changes after?

- Write the **tension** as "X, but Y". For example: "Yoga promises calm, but our phones never stop." Or: "Small businesses need video, but making it takes five tools."
- Write the **takeaway** in eight words or fewer, and the **action** (the CTA, verbatim if the client gave one).
- Find the brand's **meaning words**. Its name, product and promise usually contain a visual idea: *CALMIND* is calm + mind, so breath and quiet against noise. *Acceleration* suggests speed, vectors and momentum.

## 2. Concepts

Draft three concepts that differ in their *idea*, not their styling:

| | Title | One-sentence idea | Motion metaphor (the behaviour that is the idea) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | | | |
| B | | | |
| C | | | |

Useful idea shapes:
- **Contrast arc:** problem state, then a turn, then the resolved state. The turn is a hard reversal: a cut to silence, a colour inversion, a stop.
- **Transformation:** one object becomes the next. Use morphs and scale bridges.
- **Accumulation:** small things pile up into one big result. Use bursts, particles converging, a grid filling.
- **Journey:** one continuous line, path or camera move through the product's steps.
- **Ritual or rhythm:** the product as a repeating daily beat. Use loops, ring type and repetition.
- **Literal wordplay:** the words perform their meaning (STRETCH stretches).

Score each with [critic-rubric.md](critic-rubric.md) §1 and keep the winner. If two tie, choose the one whose ending frame is stronger.

## 3. Ending first

Write down the last 1.5–2.5 s:
- the identity: logo or wordmark, and how it arrives
- the one line
- the CTA and destination: real ones only
- what moves during the hold. Something small should keep breathing so the frame isn't dead.

Then work backwards: what does the viewer need to have seen for this frame to land?

## 4. Tempo and structure

- Pick the BPM from the emotion map. Then 1 beat = 60/BPM s, 1 bar = 4 beats; at 128 BPM a 15 s film is 8 bars exactly.
- Give each section a whole number of bars or half-bars. Bursts use half-beats; there should be at most one burst section per film.
- Draw the **contrast curve** as a row of words, for example: `LOUD-dense | SILENT-empty | building | flowing | arrival | fast-cuts | climax | hold`. If two neighbouring sections share a texture, change one.
- Place cuts on beats. Off-beat cuts are a deliberate exception and need a reason.

## 5. Shot table (fill one row per section)

| # | Section | Bars and time | Focus | Motion verb | What it means | Colour pair (bg/fg) | Type (size, weight) | Entry, then exit | Cut in, and why | Sound event |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Before building, check:
- Does every row's "what it means" connect to the insight?
- Does the colour sequence alternate light and dark often enough to punctuate, with no accidental repeats?
- Is there exactly one section where the brand is most itself, usually the ending?
- Is the CTA hold readable?

## 6. Continuity devices

- **Persistent object / match cut:** the same element survives a cut while its context changes. A device whose screen swaps. A circle that becomes a logo.
- **Callback:** something from early in the film comes back transformed. Shapes from a metaphor scene reappear as icons in the product.
- **Bookend:** the opening and ending rhyme. The same sound, shape, word or camera direction.

## 7–9. Build, look, fix

See [build-guide.md](build-guide.md) and [critic-rubric.md](critic-rubric.md). Budget at least two look-and-fix rounds. A first render always has layout bugs: overflowing text, colliding elements, edge-touching type, outline artifacts.

## Worked thinking trace (how the reasoning goes; not a visual template)

**Brief:** "Dynamic reel for CALMIND, a yoga company; it also shows the app launch; fast beats, very quick transitions."

1. **Insight:** the brand promises calm, but the client asked for speed. That *is* the tension. Tension: "Yoga is calm, but life is noise." Takeaway: "Calm is now in your pocket." Action: download the app.
2. **Concepts:**
   - A, "Noise → Breath": fast stress words and notifications, then a hard stop, one breath, and movement that becomes flow, then the app. Metaphor: speed used as the *enemy*, broken by stillness.
   - B, "One continuous breath": the whole film on one inhale/exhale curve. Calm but low-energy, so it ignores "fast beats".
   - C, "Pose alphabet": letters morph into yoga poses. Witty, but it doesn't carry the app launch.
   - A wins. It turns the client's fast-beats request into meaning, and C's pose idea can live inside it as one section.
3. **Ending:** an enso ring draws itself, the wordmark rises, "THE APP IS HERE.", the CTA, then a 1.5 s hold while the mark breathes.
4. **Tempo:** 128 BPM, 8 bars. Contrast curve: `chaos (half-beat words) | silence + breath | movement (one pose per beat) | liquid flow | phone arrives | screens per beat | half-beat burst | still hold`.
5. **Shots, one row each.** The noise words *slam* (stress). The breath is concentric rings scaled by an inhale/exhale curve (breath). The poses are one radial morph per beat (moving through a practice). The flow is metaballs that invert the type (softness, merging). The app is a phone landing with spring and blur (launch). The burst is words performing their meaning (memorability).
6. **Continuity:** the phone persists while its screens swap (match cut). The pose shapes return as icons in the app's flow screen (callback). A singing-bowl strike at the first breath and at the logo (bookend).
7. **Look-and-fix round 1 found:** notification chips colliding, ring type touching the frame edge, "OUT." overflowing its disc, and outline type showing the variable font's internal contours. All were fixed before the final render.

Notice that nothing in this trace says "like video X". Every decision comes from the brief's meaning plus the vocabulary.
