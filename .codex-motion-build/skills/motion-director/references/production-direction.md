# Production direction

Use this alongside the selected reference recipes. It adds planning and inspection detail; it does not select a renderer or add approval steps.

## Design backward from the ending

Resolve the viewer's next action, approved CTA copy and supplied destination first. Reserve a readable final hold, then choose the opening promise and product action that lead there. Do not invent a URL, offer or supporting claim to complete the ending.

Give each storyboard beat a stable scene ID and integer frame span. Record its message, approved assets, framing, entry/hold/exit, sound event and relationship to the next beat. Preserve IDs through narrow revisions; derive downstream timing from the same edit plan.

For each transition, state **what becomes what**: the outgoing object, shared visual anchor, target geometry and content overlap. Record the camera's position, framing and direction through the handover. A card becoming a grid tile should arrive while the grid forms around it. A covering shape can take on the destination background before revealing its content. Use a cut when a new thought calls for one.

Continuous moves should preserve position and velocity across joins. Check path kinks, abrupt target changes and unintended reversals; a deliberate hold or reversal remains a valid directing choice. Describe spring settlement and readable holds separately from travel.

## Inspect assets at their closest view

Measure the visible bounds of logos rather than centering transparent image padding. Check screenshots and textures at maximum planned enlargement and output resolution. Use approved vector or higher-resolution assets for details that would pixelate; a faithful rebuild needs the actual source and review. Keep UI states consistent, preserve brand geometry and remove private screenshot content using approved replacements.

## Select a treatment, not universal rules

An optional restrained product-film treatment can use coherent camera paths, subtle word reveals, limited overshoot and selective depth or lighting. Kinetic lettering, expressive springs and stronger cuts remain appropriate for their selected references. Follow the brand and message; centered framing, blur, gradients and perpetual movement are choices rather than requirements.

Existing templates demonstrate only their implemented effects. A storyboard, recipe or valid plan does not establish support for a morph, 3D camera or new text treatment. Confirm registered components or implement and test the custom effect before promising it.

Use the [production-plan contract](../starter/src/production-plan.ts) and [validation CLI](../scripts/production-plan.ts) for structured plan checks. Pair those checks with the transition and playback review in [quality-and-evals.md](quality-and-evals.md); plan validation alone cannot establish visual quality.
