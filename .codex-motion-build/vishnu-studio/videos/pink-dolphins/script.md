# Why this dolphin is pink

<!-- A+ -->
[curious] This dolphin lives in a river. It's pink. [softly] And it wasn't born that way.

[warmly] It's the Amazon river dolphin, the boto. Calves are born grey. The biggest males grow to about two
and a half metres, the largest river dolphin on Earth.

[amazed] Every rainy season the Amazon floods the forest, and the boto swims between the trees. Its neck
bones aren't fused like an ocean dolphin's, so it can turn its head ninety degrees to steer around the trunks.

<!-- B+ -->
[thoughtful] The water is too muddy to see through. So it hunts with sound, clicking up to eighty times a
second and listening for the echo off a fish.
<!-- B- -->

[mysterious] People on the river tell a story about it. [whispers] At night, the boto walks out of the water
as a handsome man. By morning, he's gone.

<!-- A- -->
[curious] So why pink? [pause] [softly] Scars. Every scrape and fight wears the grey away, and the skin heals
pink. Males fight the most, so males are the pinkest.

[sad] Today the boto is endangered, caught in fishing nets and cut off by dams. [warmly] The pinkest ones are
the ones that fought the hardest.
