- **Brand:** agency (Auralion Labs).
- **Who it is for:** owners of roofing companies (the first target, `D:\studio\agency\TARGETS.md`), and any
  local service business that lives on the phone.
- **Version 2 (2026-10-24, Vishnu's story):** after a hurricane a roofer puts a QR code on every corner of
  the roof he fixed; the neighbours scan them, every scan calls his phone, and every call gets booked
  while he's up a ladder, because Ava answers. A pure story until about 28 s, nothing that looks like an
  ad. This replaces the painted-number hook, the act timings and the stat below; the rest still holds.
- **The one point:** when the calls all come at once, a roofer up a ladder misses them. Ava answers every one.
- **The hook (first 5 seconds):** a tall tale told dead straight. "Roofers in storm country once painted
  their phone number on every roof they fixed." Strange enough that you stay for the reason.
- **Shape and length:** about 38 seconds, 9:16, animated (flat cartoon, like the reference), one narrator.
  Three acts, timed like the reference:
  1. The tale, 0 to 12 s: a strange practice, then the reason, then the problem it caused.
  2. The turn, 12 to 18 s: "But now..." The camera dives into the painted number, it becomes a call,
     and Ava answers. This is our QR scan: the story's own object carries you into the ad.
  3. The ad, 18 to 38 s: what Ava does, one real number, one call to action.
- **The metaphor or visual idea:** the phone number painted on the roof. It is the hook, the problem
  (every phone rings at once) and the door into the ad (dial it and Ava picks up).
- **The look:** act 1 is a warm flat cartoon (storm sky, a street of houses, a roofer with a paint roller).
  Acts 2 and 3 switch to the look of auralionlabs.com (`style: site`: white cards, Poppins, the orange dot),
  the way the reference switches from cartoon to its own dark interface.
- **Real numbers it uses, and where they come from:** "16 of 28 local businesses we called only reached
  voicemail" (`D:\Agency\AuralionLabs\cold-call-scripts\call-log.csv`, 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-20).
  What Ava does, in the intro video's words: answers every call, books the job into the owner's calendar,
  or takes a message and sends it on. The number on the end card is Ava's real line, +1 (415) 358-6725.
- **Left out on purpose:** prices, any client (none yet), any result for a client.
- **Honesty:** the roof story is a tall tale, like the tanks. The cartoon and the deadpan make that plain.
  Nothing in act 3 is made up.
- **The question it ends on:** none. It ends on the call to action: "Call Ava yourself."

## What makes the reference work (measured 2026-10-24 from the downloaded reel)

- **Words:** 95 words in 37 s. A fact-shaped claim ("soldiers once..."), then "because", then "But now,"
  at 10 s, the turn into the ad from 10 to 17 s, three concrete chores, one offer, one call to action.
- **Music:** the loudness never moves (about -21 dB all the way). The suspense comes from texture, not volume:
  - 0 to 8 s: no sub-bass, bright and sparse.
  - At 8 s, two seconds before "But now": deep sub-bass comes in (about 10 dB more below 120 Hz).
  - 10 to 19 s, the turn: the busiest part, the most hits per second.
  - 22 to 33 s, under the offer: it thins out so the voice carries.
  - The end: thin and bright, a short tail.
  So: bass in just before the twist, rhythm peaks on the reveal, then it gets out of the way.
- **Captions:** one or two words at a time, white bold capitals in a black box; the brand words
  ("TASKLESS", "ONLY AT TASKLESS") in the brand colour.
