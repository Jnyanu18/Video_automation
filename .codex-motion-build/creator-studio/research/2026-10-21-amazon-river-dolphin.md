# Amazon river dolphin (boto): facts for `pink-dolphins`

Checked 2026-10-21. Only what is here goes into the script.

| Claim | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Born dark grey, light grey as a juvenile, pink as an adult | Wikipedia, Amazon river dolphin |
| Pink comes from repeated scraping of the skin (scar tissue); males are pinker because they fight more | Wikipedia; Natural Habitat Adventures blog says the same |
| Largest river dolphin; males up to 2.55 m and 185 kg | Wikipedia |
| Neck vertebrae are not fused, so it can turn its head 90 degrees | Wikipedia (one blog says 180; not used) |
| Steers through flooded forest in the rainy season, slower than ocean dolphins | Wikipedia; National Geographic |
| Murky water, so it hunts mostly by echolocation, 30 to 80 clicks a second | Wikipedia |
| Legend: the encantado, a dolphin that becomes a handsome man at night and returns to the river by morning | Wikipedia |
| IUCN Endangered since 2018, population declining | Wikipedia (cites the IUCN assessment) |
| Threats: fishing nets, killing for bait, dams, pollution | Wikipedia |

Not used: "river dolphins down 73% since the 1980s" (one WWF figure seen second hand; not checked
against WWF) and the 2023 Lake Tefé deaths (true, but a second story in a 50-second video).
