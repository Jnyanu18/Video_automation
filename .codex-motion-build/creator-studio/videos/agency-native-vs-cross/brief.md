# Brief: Your app doesn't need to be built twice

- **Brand:** agency (Auralion Labs). Final goes to `agency/videos/`, not `publish/`.
- **Funnel:** top.
- **Who it is for:** founders and business owners planning an app who assume iOS plus Android means two builds.
- **The one point:** most apps can be built once for both platforms; go native only when the hardware is the product.
- **Reference:** none; an idea from the agency list (2026-11-08), Vishnu: "I love it, and the premium UI as well".
- **Purple cow:** the first line undoes the assumption the viewer walked in with ("built twice"). Shows at: frame 0, one
  phone splitting into two, then snapping back into one. Pays off at: "build once". Story-loops: A+ "doesn't need to be
  built twice" / B+ "So when do you still go native?" closed at B- "When hardware is the product" / A- "Simple as that".
- **Look:** premium UI (Vishnu's word) in the website's black and white: a dark stage, two real-looking phones (iPhone
  and Android) with polished app screens (sign in, a booking, a payment), the framework and language logos as real
  assets (React Native, Flutter, Swift, Kotlin), springy entrances, a slow camera push. Mocks labelled ILLUSTRATIVE.
- **Facts and sources:** all from our post /blog/native-vs-cross-platform-app-2026 and the frameworks' own descriptions:
  React Native and Flutter ship iOS and Android from one codebase; Swift and Kotlin are the native languages; camera, AR,
  Bluetooth, widgets and watch apps are where native earns its cost. No numbers.
- **Call to action:** comment STACK, which DMs the decision checklist as a .docx guide (`dm-guide.md`, then
  `kit/guide_docx.py`), built from the blog post's checklist.
- **Voice:** to decide: Christopher (the faceless reels' voice, ElevenLabs credits) or Vishnu records it.
- **Music:** a library track Vishnu picks. No ElevenLabs music.
- **Direction:** below (2026-11-08). Timings are estimates from the script (no voice yet: ElevenLabs key 2 had 541
  credits left, the script needs about 521; key 1 returns 401).
