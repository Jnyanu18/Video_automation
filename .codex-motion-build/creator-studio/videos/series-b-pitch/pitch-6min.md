# AskVerb: the 6-minute verbal pitch (2026-10-24)

<!-- Spoken, nothing on screen, to founders who have raised a Series B. 6 minutes of pitch, then 4 of questions.
About 690 words: about 5 minutes at 140 a minute, which leaves a minute for pauses and nerves. No em dashes. Each section has its time. -->

## 0:00 The problem (about 45 seconds)

Think about the last time you used a product and knew a feature existed, but couldn't find it.

You clicked around. You opened the docs. Maybe you asked support. And then you gave up.

Every product in this room has that moment. Your users know what they want to do. They just can't get the product to do it fast enough. So they stall, they open a ticket, or they leave.

You've spent years building those features. And a lot of your users never reach them. Not because the feature is bad, but because finding it takes more effort than they're willing to give on a busy day.

## 0:45 What AskVerb is (about 1 minute)

AskVerb gives your product its own AI agent.

It sits inside your app. It's already signed in as the user, so it sees exactly what they're allowed to see, and nothing more. The user just types what they want: "move everything due this week to Monday and tell the team."

The agent works out the steps, shows the user exactly what it's about to change, and when they say yes, it does it, in their account, using the actions your product already has.

Or in an invoicing product: "send a reminder to every client who hasn't paid this month." Today that's a filter, a list, and twenty emails. With AskVerb, it's one sentence and one yes.

So the user doesn't learn your interface. They ask, and it gets done.

## 1:45 How it works for a team like yours (about 1 minute)

You don't rebuild anything.

First, it reads your website, your pricing, your FAQ, your docs, and builds a read-only assistant in a couple of minutes, so you can see it answer correctly before it touches anything.

Then you decide what it's allowed to do. You list those actions in one file. Your own coding agent can write it, through our MCP. Most teams are live in under an hour.

And every action it takes asks the user first, stays inside that user's permissions, and is written down, so you always know what it did and why.

## 2:45 Why now (about 30 seconds)

Two things changed. Models can now use tools reliably, which they couldn't two years ago. And users have learned to ask instead of click. They do it everywhere else now, and they're starting to expect it inside the products they pay for.

## 3:15 Why me (about 45 seconds)

I'm 22. I've shipped more than six apps. One of them has over 2,500 users and makes around 100 dollars a month. Small, but it taught me what users actually do in a product, and where they get stuck.

I also run an agency that builds AI agents for clients. So I've built this kind of agent by hand, again and again, and I've seen the same hard part every time. The model is easy. The permissions, the confirmation, and knowing what it did are hard. AskVerb is that hard part, built once.

## 4:00 Where I am, honestly (about 45 seconds)

I'm on day 24 of a 30-day challenge to get my first ten paying customers, in public.

I have about 23 sign-ups. I have zero paying customers.

When I asked people why they signed up and didn't install it, the answer was clear. It wasn't the number of steps. It's that putting something into your live product needs a reason bigger than curiosity, and often someone else's approval. So I'm changing the order: show the agent working on your own product first, then ask you to install it.

## 4:45 The market and how it grows (about 45 seconds)

Every SaaS with real users and a workflow people get stuck in is a customer.

It scales because there's no custom work per customer. One snippet, one file, and the agent uses what the product already does.

I start with a free 30-day pilot on one workflow, and we agree the price after, from what it actually handled. It grows through the products themselves: every user who gets something done by asking is the demo for the next founder.

## 5:30 The ask (about 30 seconds)

So here's my ask.

If your product has a workflow your users get stuck in, give me that one workflow. I'll run a 30-day pilot on it, and we'll measure what it handles.

And if it's not for you, tell me which founder you know who has that problem.

I'm Vishnu. This is AskVerb. Thank you.

## For the 4 minutes of questions

Short answers, then stop. Likely questions:

- **"Why won't we just build this ourselves?"** You could build the agent in a few weeks. The part that takes a quarter is permissions, confirming every change, and the record of what it did. That's what AskVerb is.
- **"What if it does something wrong?"** It can only do what you listed, only inside the user's own permissions, and it asks before every change. Everything it did is written down.
- **"How is it different from a chatbot?"** Don't compare. Give the example again: it's signed in as the user, it uses the product's own actions, and the tasks get moved.
- **"What's your traction?"** The real numbers: about 23 sign-ups, 0 paying, day 24, and what I learned about why.
- **"What do you charge?"** A free 30-day pilot on one workflow, then a price we agree from what it handled.
- **"Are you raising?"** Answer only what is true today. If you are not raising, say you're focused on the first ten customers.
