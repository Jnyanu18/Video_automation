Every chat your AI bot has could be worth five thousand dollars. To the person suing you.

Air Canada's chatbot made up a refund policy, and a customer believed it. A tribunal ruled the bot's words were the airline's words. Air Canada paid.

In California, websites whose chat widgets send transcripts to a vendor got hit with dozens of wiretap lawsuits. Each one asks five thousand dollars per violation.

The FTC made DoNotPay pay a hundred and ninety-three thousand dollars. Its AI was sold as a lawyer. Nobody had tested that.

And since August, the EU AI Act says your bot has to tell people it's an AI, from the first message. Big companies face up to fifteen million euros. Start-ups, up to three percent of revenue.

Every reply your bot writes, you signed.

So make it say it's an AI. Let it answer only from your real policies. Ask before you record a chat. Claim only what you've tested. Four lines in a prompt.
